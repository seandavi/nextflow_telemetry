# Research: SingleM and Sandpiper, and what they mean for our MetaPhlAn-based corpus

*Researched 2026-10-15. Primary sources: the SingleM repo and docs, the Sandpiper site (read via a headless browser, since the pages are client-rendered), the Sandpiper Zenodo record, the MetaPhlAn repo, and PubMed. Repo inputs: `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/roadmap.md`, `docs/output-catalog-etl-design.md`, `docs/storage-layout.md`, `docs/sample-metadata-design.md`. The Nature Biotechnology paper is paywalled and the bioRxiv preprint returned 403/429, so **benchmark numbers from the paper are not reproduced here** and are marked UNVERIFIED where they matter. Anything not marked UNVERIFIED was read from the cited page.*

## Summary

- **SingleM** profiles a metagenome from 20-amino-acid windows of 59 single-copy marker genes (35 bacterial, 37 archaeal, per Sandpiper's About page). It clusters the windows into OTUs before assigning taxonomy, so lineages with no genome in the reference can still be counted. It is GPL-3.0, current release v0.21.4 (2026-08-12), and default taxonomy is GTDB R226.
- **Sandpiper** is the SRA-wide application: 913,071 metagenomes screened, 6,378 Tbp, 42,716 projects (site home page, v2.1.1). Profiles are bulk-downloadable from Zenodo under CC-BY-4.0. Sandpiper 2.1.1 uses **GTDB R232** (and GlobDB R232).
- **Our output is directly comparable after a deterministic relabel.** MetaPhlAn ships SGB-to-GTDB maps in its repo: `mpa_vJan25_..._SGB2GTDB_r220.tsv` and `mpa_vJan26_..._SGB2GTDB_r226.tsv`. Our pipeline already emits a `gtdb_profile` per sample. The release mismatch (R220 or R226 on our side, R232 on Sandpiper's) is the main thing to handle in a comparison.
- **Abundances are not the same quantity.** Sandpiper reports per-taxon *coverage*, and relative abundance is coverage divided by total root coverage. MetaPhlAn's `relative_abundance` is a percent of a marker-normalized profile that includes an `UNCLASSIFIED` row. Compare at genus level on renormalized, classified-only profiles, never at raw species level.
- **Recommendation:** use Sandpiper as an external reference and sample-selection aid, and run a small bounded validation on overlapping SRR accessions. Do not add SingleM to the production pipeline yet.

## 1. What SingleM does

- **Method.** "It targets 20-amino-acid windows within single-copy marker genes, then uses those to estimate lineage abundance." Originally built for bacteria and archaea; dsDNA phage profiling (Lyrebird) arrived in v0.19.0 and long-read input in v0.20.0. [docs home](https://wwood.github.io/singlem/), [repo README](https://github.com/wwood/singlem)
- **Marker count.** 59 genes total (35 bacterial, 37 archaeal). Reads covering the conserved windows are clustered "prior to taxonomy assignment, allowing detection of strain-level diversity and novel lineages." [Sandpiper About](https://sandpiper.qut.edu.au/About)
- **Subcommands.** `pipe` (main workflow: OTU tables and GTDB profiles), `summarise` (mechanical transforms of `pipe` output), `condense` (advanced: OTU table to taxonomic profile), `appraise` (how much of a metagenome a set of genomes or an assembly represents), `prokaryotic_fraction` (formerly `microbial_fraction`), `data`, `renew`, `supplement`. [docs home](https://wwood.github.io/singlem/)
- **Pipeline inside `pipe`.** Windows are matched to GTDB species-representative windows (within 96.7% identity, or 2 bp over the 60 bp window); unmatched ones fall back to DIAMOND blastx. Default `--assignment-method` is `smafa_naive_then_diamond`. The last step, "condense", combines the 59 per-marker results using trimmed means (default 10% trimmed per taxon) and expectation maximisation; taxa below `--min-taxon-coverage` 0.35 are zeroed. [pipe](https://wwood.github.io/singlem/tools/pipe), [condense](https://wwood.github.io/singlem/advanced/condense)
- **Outputs.** `-p` condensed taxonomic profile, `--taxonomic-profile-krona`, `--otu-table`, `--archive-otu-table` (for building databases). The docs pages do not give the column layout of the profile. The Sandpiper bulk file shows it (section 3). [pipe](https://wwood.github.io/singlem/tools/pipe)
- **Abundance fields.** `num_hits` is the reads assigned to an OTU. `coverage = num_hits * L / (L - k + 1)`, with L the read length and k the OTU sequence length (usually 60 bp). [FAQ](https://wwood.github.io/singlem/FAQ)
- **Metapackages and taxonomy.** The docs say S4.x and S5.x metapackages cover GTDB 07-RS207 up to the default, GTDB R226 "at the time of writing", distributed as versions of one Zenodo record. Sandpiper's changelog names a concrete one: `S5.4.0.GTDB_r226.metapackage_20250331.smpkg`. The docs do not map every S4.x/S5.x version to a GTDB release, and give no download size. A separate GlobDB metapackage (many more genomes than GTDB) is available. [data](https://wwood.github.io/singlem/tools/data), [Sandpiper About](https://sandpiper.qut.edu.au/About)
- **Install.** Bioconda, Docker/Apptainer (`wwood/singlem:0.21.4`, reference data included in the image), PyPI, or pixi from source. [Installation](https://wwood.github.io/singlem/Installation)
- **Cost.** `pipe` "should run in under 4 GB of RAM by default, except for very large metagenomes (over 100 Gbp)"; `--threads` defaults to 1. No runtime figures are published on the docs pages I read; runtime per sample is **UNVERIFIED**. For scale, our MetaPhlAn task needs ~38 GiB RSS because of the bowtie2 index (`docs/research/resource-tuning-2.3.0.md`), so a SingleM step would be far smaller in memory. [pipe](https://wwood.github.io/singlem/tools/pipe)
- **Prokaryotic fraction (SPF).** Estimates the share of reads (as bases) from bacteria or archaea, plus average genome size, with no eukaryote or host reference needed. It sums taxon genome sizes times coverage and divides by metagenome size. TSV columns include `read_fraction` (a percent), `bacterial_archaeal_bases`, `metagenome_size` and `average_bacterial_archaeal_genome_size`. It is least reliable for simple communities of species missing from the database. The page itself uses "prokaryotic fraction", not "bacterial fraction". [prokaryotic_fraction](https://wwood.github.io/singlem/tools/prokaryotic_fraction); citation Eisenhofer, Alberdi and Woodcroft, mSystems 11(4), e0106225, [doi:10.1128/msystems.01062-25](https://doi.org/10.1128/msystems.01062-25).
- **Licence.** GPL-3.0 (GitHub API license field for `wwood/singlem`; also stated on the [repo README](https://github.com/wwood/singlem)). This matters only if we link or redistribute the code. Running it as a separate container step does not impose terms on our pipeline, but redistributing a modified SingleM would.

## 2. Where it is strong, and how it differs from MetaPhlAn

- **Novel and uncultured lineages.** The preprint argues MetaPhlAn 4's reference, even with MAGs, "only includes genomes which are currently assembled and of medium-to-high quality." MetaPhlAn 4 has 26,970 SGBs, 4,992 of them uncharacterized (uSGBs), per the MetaPhlAn 4 paper (as quoted from search results; I did not open that paper). A conference abstract for SingleM reports that most marine, freshwater, sediment and soil metagenomes are dominated by species without genomic representation (median relative abundance 75.0%). That is **UNVERIFIED against the peer-reviewed paper**. [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.01.30.578060.full.pdf), [conference abstract](https://asmicro-2024.p.asnevents.com.au/days/2024-07-02/abstract/104563)
- **Paper.** Woodcroft BJ, Aroney STN, Zhao R, Cunningham M, Mitchell JAM, Nurdiansyah R, Blackall L, Tyson GW. *Nat Biotechnol* 44(6):948-953 (2025), [doi:10.1038/s41587-025-02738-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-025-02738-1) (PMID 40670710, via PubMed). The abstract says SingleM "estimates community composition using conserved regions within universal marker genes" and that Sandpiper "collates microbial community profiles from 248,559 publicly available metagenomes" (the count at publication; the site has grown since).
- **Head-to-head accuracy vs MetaPhlAn 4.** The paper's benchmark results are **UNVERIFIED** here (paywall and preprint blocked). Do not cite a direction or magnitude until someone reads the full text.
- **For human gut samples**, where MetaPhlAn's database is densest, the two profilers should agree best. The novelty advantage matters most for non-gut and poorly characterized habitats. This is my inference from the novelty argument, not a measured result.

### Abundance semantics

| | SingleM / Sandpiper | MetaPhlAn (our `cmgd_mpa4.2`) |
|---|---|---|
| Native quantity | Per-taxon `coverage` (expected genome coverage derived from marker-window read hits) | `relative_abundance` (percent), plus `coverage` and `est_reads` columns in our `marker_rel_ab_w_read_stats` |
| Relative abundance | taxon coverage divided by the total root coverage ([Sandpiper About](https://sandpiper.qut.edu.au/About)) | percent of a profile that includes an `UNCLASSIFIED` remainder (`docs/output-catalog-etl-design.md`) |
| Denominator | Prokaryotic marker coverage. Eukaryote and host reads sit outside it. SPF reports that share separately | Marker/genome-size-normalized; includes an unclassified fraction |
| Cell vs read | Coverage of single-copy genes is proportional to genome (cell) count, so it behaves like a cell-equivalent measure. This is my inference; the docs I read do not say "cell fraction" ([FAQ](https://wwood.github.io/singlem/FAQ) is silent) | Same inference (marker-coverage-based), and our design doc calls it "marker/genome-size-normalized". Read-fraction is a separate field, `est_reads` |
| Novel taxa | Counted, assigned at the lowest resolvable rank | Only if the lineage has an SGB, including uSGBs |

Practical consequence: summing to 100% means different things on each side. Renormalize both to classified-at-genus before comparing.

## 3. Sandpiper

- **Coverage.** Home page (v2.1.1): "913,071 metagenomes screened. 6,378 Tbp and 42,716 projects." Source list is SRA runs designated metagenomic (or from "metagenomic" organisms), generated 2026-03-03. Metadata annotations were pulled with Kingfisher on 2026-05-14. Release v2.0 added 205,695 metagenomes. [home](https://sandpiper.qut.edu.au/), [About](https://sandpiper.qut.edu.au/About)
- **Site URL.** The URL in the task brief (`sandpiper.qut.ecogenomic.org`) does not resolve (DNS ENOTFOUND). The live site is `https://sandpiper.qut.edu.au/`.
- **Cadence.** Irregular, driven by releases: v0.2 (Dec 2021 SRA list), v0.3 (GTDB R220), v1.0 (SRA to 2025-02-20, GTDB R226, SingleM 0.18.3), v1.1 (GlobDB R226), v2.0 (GTDB R232, SRA to 2026-03-03), v2.1 (GlobDB R232). Roughly one to two releases a year. [About changelog](https://sandpiper.qut.edu.au/About)
- **Taxonomy.** Current: GTDB R232 and GlobDB R232. Previous: GTDB R226 (v1.0.x). Version numbers are shown on every database reference.
- **Per-run derived fields.** Low-complexity flag (95% or more of the community in a single order), SPF and average genome size, `known_species_fraction`, host-associated vs ecological prediction (XGBoost, about 93% accuracy against NCBI "organism"), and flags for non-metagenome organism, synthetic, RNA/non-DNA and domain-only. [About](https://sandpiper.qut.edu.au/About)
- **Bulk download.** Zenodo concept DOI [10.5281/zenodo.10547493](https://zenodo.org/doi/10.5281/zenodo.10547493), licence **CC-BY-4.0**; older Sandpiper versions are kept as old Zenodo versions. The record's latest listing (version 2.1.0, 2026-07-10) has:
  - `sandpiper2.0.1.gtdb.csv.gz` (3.3 GB). Long table with header `sample, coverage, taxonomy`, e.g. `ERR1301040, 2.32, "Root; d__Bacteria"`. One row per sample and taxon, with the full lineage in a single string.
  - `sandpiper2.1.0.globdb.csv.gz` (3.5 GB), same layout on GlobDB.
  - `sandpiper2.0.1.per_acc_summary.csv.gz` (40 MB). One row per run with `root_coverage, species_coverage, top1_order_fraction, top3_order_fraction, low_complexity, known_species_fraction, bacterial_archaeal_bases, metagenome_size, singlem_prokaryotic_fraction, average_bacterial_archaeal_genome_size, warning, prediction, host_or_not, organism`.
  - `sandpiper2.0.1.parsed_metadata.tsv.gz` and `sandpiper2.1.0.kingfisher_metadata.tsv.gz`.
  - I read headers and the first rows only, from the `2.0.1` files. The file names carry different version numbers within one record. I did not download the full files.
- **API.** None found. The site lists "Search", the downloads are the programmatic route. OTU tables can no longer be downloaded from the site (v1.1 changelog); the About page says per-run raw profiles were once available. Anything beyond the Zenodo files is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Limitations stated by the authors.** Many SRA metadata fields are wrong or missing (they keep a public corrections repo). Low-complexity runs may be SAGs, controls or isolates mislabelled as metagenomes. Bespoke analyses with alternative groupings or novel lineages "cannot currently be done on the sandpiper website." [About](https://sandpiper.qut.edu.au/About)
- **Olm Lab Microbiome Repo.** The repo's downloads page lists Sandpiper-derived tables: `gut_sandpiper_sample_summary.parquet` (339,626 rows), `gut_sandpiper_study_panels.parquet` (per-project mean top-12 genera), PCA scores/loadings/variance, `gut_sandpiper_study_coverage.csv`, and for infant studies `sandpiper_profiles.parquet`, `sandpiper_run_qc.parquet` and QC-flag tables. Its R2026.25 release notes list Sandpiper 2.0.0 and report 79,473 profiled samples. The page I read gives no GTDB release or derivation method; those live in `SANDPIPER_REPORT.md`, which I did not open. [Olm repo downloads](https://olmlab.github.io/microbiome_repo/downloads/index.html). So the Olm repo is a downstream consumer that joins Sandpiper profiles onto its own gut-sample catalog; it is a second place our samples could meet Sandpiper numbers.

## 4. What we produce (for comparison)

- Pipeline `cmgd_nextflow`; per sample, per branch (`full_data/`, `rarefied_data/` at 1M reads, seed 42): MetaPhlAn `marker_rel_ab_w_read_stats`, `marker_abundance`, `marker_presence`, StrainPhlAn `metaphlan.json`; Kraken2/Bracken species and genus; `gtdb_profile`; resistome; FastQC; `manifest.json` with `read_accounting`, `parameters.metaphlan_index`, `software_versions` (`docs/output-catalog-etl-design.md`).
- Current index per the brief: MetaPhlAn 4.2.6, vJan26. Earlier runs used vJan25 (`mpa_vJan25_CHOCOPhlAnSGB_202503`). The sample key `meta.sample` is `md5(sorted SRR accessions)`, so a sample may bundle several runs.
- **MetaPhlAn-to-GTDB.** `metaphlan/utils/sgb_to_gtdb_profile.py` (flags `-i`, `-o`, `--gtdb_assignment_file`, `--merged_profiles`) auto-detects `<mpa_index>_SGB2GTDB*.tsv` next to the script (SGB id in column 1, GTDB taxonomy in column 2). It sums SGBs per GTDB species, renormalizes species to 100 minus unclassified, and builds higher ranks by summing children. SGBs without a mapping are skipped. [script](https://github.com/biobakery/MetaPhlAn/blob/master/metaphlan/utils/sgb_to_gtdb_profile.py)
- **Release of the maps** (file listing of `metaphlan/utils` in the MetaPhlAn repo): `mpa_vJan25_CHOCOPhlAnSGB_202503_SGB2GTDB_r220.tsv` and `mpa_vJan26_CHOCOPhlAnSGB_202605_SGB2GTDB_r226.tsv`. So vJan25 maps to **GTDB r220** and vJan26 to **r226**. MetaPhlAn 4.2.6 was released 2026-07-28 ([releases](https://github.com/biobakery/MetaPhlAn/releases)).
- Our `gtdb_profile` is the same relabel, produced in-pipeline. The design doc proposes dropping that step and deriving it by join; the Sandpiper comparison is a second reason to keep a version-pinned SGB-to-GTDB `taxon` dimension.

## How we can leverage this

1. **Side-by-side GTDB comparison (cheap, do first).** Our `gtdb_profile` (or a rebuilt one from `t__SGB` rows plus the `r220`/`r226` map) can be compared with Sandpiper's GTDB profile at genus and family. The release gap is the caveat: Sandpiper is R232, we are R220 (vJan25) or R226 (vJan26). Compare at genus and above, and treat renamed or split genera as noise. A GTDB R226-to-R232 name crosswalk is **UNVERIFIED**; GlobDB R226 data from Sandpiper v1.1 is a near release match for vJan26 but the Zenodo record now lists the R232 version.
2. **Sandpiper as a pre-screen and sample-selection aid.** `per_acc_summary` gives, per SRR: `host_or_not`/`prediction`, `low_complexity`, `singlem_prokaryotic_fraction`, `known_species_fraction`, `organism`. Use it before we spend about 14 CPU-h per sample (`resource-tuning-2.3.0.md`) to (a) skip runs flagged low-complexity or non-DNA, (b) prefer runs with high prokaryotic fraction (a low fraction means few usable reads after host removal), and (c) find gut, human-associated runs outside the cMD set. Treat the host prediction as a hint, not a label (about 93% accuracy per the authors). It joins on SRR, which we hold in `manifest.json` `provenance.input_ids`.
3. **Cross-validation experiment on overlapping runs.**
   - *Population:* single-run samples (one SRR per `meta.sample`) present in both our catalog and Sandpiper, so no run aggregation is needed. Start with 200 to 500 runs across at least 10 studies, mixing gut and non-gut if we have any.
   - *Our side:* the `full_data` GTDB profile at genus (and family), renormalized to the classified portion. Record `parameters.metaphlan_index` per sample so vJan25 and vJan26 runs are not pooled.
   - *Sandpiper side:* rows from `sandpiper*.gtdb.csv.gz` filtered to those SRRs; take genus-rank lineages, convert coverage to relative abundance by dividing by root coverage, then renormalize to classified-at-genus.
   - *Metrics:* per-sample Bray-Curtis dissimilarity at genus (primary); Spearman correlation of genus abundances; fraction of our top-10 genera in Sandpiper's top-10; presence/absence agreement (Jaccard) at a 0.01% threshold; and the share of Sandpiper coverage in "unmapped" lineages (low `known_species_fraction`) as the explanation for the biggest disagreements.
   - *Controls:* compare `full_data` vs `rarefied_data` Bray-Curtis within our own pipeline to set a floor for "same method, less depth". Note read depth differs: Sandpiper profiles the full run, our rarefied branch uses 1M reads.
   - *Reading the result:* a high median similarity on gut samples validates both. Low similarity concentrated in low-`known_species_fraction` samples says MetaPhlAn is missing novel lineages. Low similarity in well-characterized gut samples points to a mapping or normalization problem; check GTDB renames first.
   - *Cost:* the Zenodo GTDB file is 3.3 GB compressed. Filter to our SRRs once with DuckDB and keep only the subset.
4. **SingleM as a complementary profiler in our pipeline (defer).** It would add a second taxonomic view that includes novel lineages and the SPF stat, at low memory. Against that: another tool and metapackage to version, the `pipe` runtime per sample is **UNVERIFIED**, and we would re-run every sample or add a new workflow version, which interacts with the "exactly one active version per workflow" rule in `docs/roadmap.md`. Trial it on the validation subset from item 3 first, as a one-off job, and decide from the numbers.
5. **Catalog fit.** If we do ingest SingleM or Sandpiper numbers, put them in their own table (`taxonomic_profile_singlem`), not merged with MetaPhlAn or Bracken. The design doc already rejects a unified table because the abundance columns carry different interpretations, and Sandpiper's `coverage` is a third one.
6. **Licensing and citation.** Sandpiper data: CC-BY-4.0, so redistribution within a derived table is fine with attribution. SingleM code: GPL-3.0. Cite Woodcroft et al. 2025 (doi above) for any use of SingleM or Sandpiper profiles, and Eisenhofer et al. 2026 for SPF. Record Sandpiper version and GTDB release in every derived artifact, alongside the Zenodo version DOI.

## How we are differentiated

- **We run the profiler per sample with provenance; Sandpiper publishes results.** Every sample has `manifest.json` with pipeline version, git commit, command line, MetaPhlAn index, `read_accounting`, and tool versions (`docs/output-catalog-etl-design.md`). Sandpiper's profiles carry a Sandpiper release and database version but, from what I read, no per-run software or parameter record. That would be **UNVERIFIED** for the internal Sandpiper pipeline.
- **Versioned, re-runnable workflow.** Workflow revisions are registered with one active version per workflow, completion is counted in distinct samples under the active version, and DLQ/retry logic is built in (`CLAUDE.md`, `docs/roadmap.md`). Sandpiper republishes whole releases.
- **Pull-mode HPC orchestration.** Daemons on Alpine and Anvil claim jobs and submit to SLURM; the Worker at `nf-telemetry.seandavi.workers.dev` holds state (`CLAUDE.md`). This is how we process our own corpus, and it also lets us reprocess a chosen subset on demand. Sandpiper's infrastructure was funded and run on AWS/GCP (Acknowledgements).
- **Two branches per sample** (full and 1M-read rarefied), which gives a depth-sensitivity measurement per sample. Sandpiper has one profile per run.
- **Outputs beyond a taxonomic profile.** Marker-level abundance and presence, StrainPhlAn marker data (`metaphlan.json`) for strain-level work, Kraken2/Bracken as an independent classifier, resistome (KMA/CARD), and HUMAnN gene families and pathways planned for 2.3.0 (`docs/research/results-storage-and-access.md`). SingleM keeps OTU window sequences, and I found nothing equivalent to strain-level SNV profiling. Whether SingleM OTUs support strain tracking is **UNVERIFIED**; its docs claim "more strain-level resolution" from protein-coding windows ([FAQ](https://wwood.github.io/singlem/FAQ)).
- **Output catalog on DuckLake/R2.** Tidy Parquet, one catalog, public read-only access over HTTPS, versioned snapshots, SQL across profilers with a `taxon` crosswalk dimension (`docs/output-catalog-etl-design.md`, `docs/storage-layout.md`). Sandpiper offers a search UI and flat CSV downloads (3 to 4 GB each) on Zenodo.
- **Curated cMD-style metadata and collections.** Collections are first-class (ADR-0005), with curated annotations stored separately from SRA-derived metadata (`docs/sample-metadata-design.md`). Sandpiper's metadata is SRA-derived with ML-predicted host labels and a corrections repo.

**Where Sandpiper is ahead.**
- **Scale of coverage:** 913,071 runs screened and 42,716 projects, versus our corpus of a few thousand processed samples so far (~1,727 on the telemetry DB per project notes; planning targets go higher). Our corpus is a curated subset by design.
- **Public search UI:** taxon search, random-sample draws, map coordinates, and per-run pages. Our dashboard is an operator view; the public query surface is still being built.
- **Novelty handling:** profiles lineages that have no genome. We cannot, without a reference.
- **Low-cost method:** about 4 GB of RAM against our ~38 GiB MetaPhlAn tasks.
- **Peer-reviewed benchmark and community adoption:** Nat Biotechnol 2025, and downstream use such as the Olm Lab repo.
